Born out of the 6-month retrospective and the H2 planning session with Cao Phong on 8 May 2026. No targets were set in advance, so there is nothing to measure against — this section records delivered results only, and is deliberately excluded from the 73% headline so it neither inflates nor deflates the scored cycle. Most of Tam's largest work between May and August lives here rather than in the nine KRs above. Tam's own framing of the period: "a shift from learning to product — the Claude Architect material moved off the page and into working tools."

From September this becomes a scored objective. It ran this cycle without targets because it emerged mid-flight; that was the right call for six months of exploration and would be the wrong call for the next six. The results below are what it has to show for the period.
🧠 UnIqueS Brain — shared knowledge base

Built as an Obsidian + Git wiki for the core team: a single place where what the team knows lives, instead of scattering across chats and drives.

Next: Map of Content, a dashboard, and mirroring GitLab → GitHub so it can be used in Claude.ai and shared with the team.
🤖 Unis — the UnIqueS brain bot

A working RAG-based assistant, built to test how the Claude Architect course material holds up in practice: design trade-offs between cost, corpus size, effectiveness, and model limits. One month of research into "second brain" applied to organisational knowledge management. Presented in a sharing session with a bilingual reference document and a recorded demo.

Next: run the bot in Google Chat and Slack · demo to PNV and propose a pilot · demo requested for Impact Hub APAC (Xuan Ngan).
🎓 UnIqueS Academy — academy.uniques.vn (shipped)

A learning platform built to support the CCAF journey, grounded in learning science and positioning the chatbot as a tutor rather than an answer machine. Nine principles including Productive Struggle First, Retrieval Practice, and Socratic mode. First module: Building with the Claude API. Prompt Evaluation was applied while designing the workflow, and a Definition of Done was created to hold the quality bar.

Next: modules beyond Module 1, after the Prompting session.
🛠 New SDLC — how AI changes each phase

Tam's way of instructing Claude Code shifted toward Scrum and software-development frameworks — more control over what AI suggests and over the consequences of its output. Research complete and documented in July. Reinforced by earning the PSM certificate: Tam now works as Product Owner to an AI "developer", with a Product Goal, a Product Backlog, a Definition of Done, and weekly retrospectives whose improvements feed the next iteration. Related public thinking: the "vibe coding vs agentic engineering" piece, arguing that without tests and evals it is vibe coding no matter how good the prompt.

Next: apply each phase to development with Claude · share the knowledge in August.
🌐 RO LLM Wiki — knowledge graph complete

Built to preserve and reorganise the knowledge of the global Reinventing Organizations (Teal) community. The knowledge graph was completed in July.

Next: share with the RO community in August.
💎 Unis the Phoenix — mascot chosen

The Phoenix, named Unis, adopted as the UnIqueS mascot — symbolising rebirth and a deliberate soft-skills counterweight to the technology side of the work.

🏪 Ecosystem strategy — the reframe

Two decisions in July moved UnIqueS from "AI training vendor" toward "ecosystem contributor". First, the Olivier Lefèvre meeting (7 Jul) reframed the relationship from buying/selling AI training to Olivier joining the wider UnIqueS ecosystem. Second, UnIqueS began contributing VND 2m/month back to Impact Hub Saigon — symbolic rather than material, and explicitly about showing up as a contributing member rather than only a beneficiary.

Surfaced a new marketplace project idea, possibly built with Diane or others practising what they learn on the CCAF journey. Parking lot: a 20th-anniversary unconference, and a monthly podcast with French-based coaches and facilitators.
🔬 Science Fair 2026 — two proposals submitted

Two bilingual (VN/EN) submissions prepared in August, one of them on Knowledge Management — putting the UnIqueS Brain and Unis bot work in front of an external audience. Submitted; no result at cycle close.

🏠 Cosmic House — "Third Space" community hub

A hybrid live/work community hub in Ho Chi Minh City blending personal development, trust and community. Project kicked off and running weekly, and formalised as a partnership in August. What the six months produced

  • A signed Anthropic partnership, end to end. Application (1 Apr) → review passed → team onboarded → Partner Network Agreement signed (late May) → licensing settled on Max → first CCAF certification (Aug). From nothing to a contracted partner with a certified practitioner in five months.
  • External delivery tripled. One programme in May became three: Impact Hub Saigon, PNV-Teachers (AI), PNV-Students (NVC). Three other pipeline routes closed along the way and were replaced by ones that converted.
  • Reach roughly doubled on every channel. LinkedIn 29 → 49 followers and 31 → 64 posts; Facebook 9 → 30 followers. Best post of the cycle: 22 May, 396 impressions.
  • The website was rebuilt and the catalog delivered — structure, content, and a two-format brochure. KR 3.2 closed at 100%.
  • Public workshops went from zero to two, and the second one — Flow Game × Impact Hub — became a recurring monthly community offering rather than a one-off.
  • And outside the scored set entirely: UnIqueS Brain, the Unis chatbot, UnIqueS Academy, the RO LLM Wiki knowledge graph, the New SDLC research, and a PSM certification applied directly to how Tam works with AI. Most of the cycle's largest work sits here.

What was missed, and by how much

  • KR 1.1 — 11 of 24 sessions (46%). Under half the target. Actual pace ~1.8 sessions/month against a target of 4.
  • KR 1.2 — missed its 21 May deadline and closed at 70%. Workflow 2 was never started; the automation half of this KR did not move once between March and the end of the cycle.
  • KR 2.3 — 1 of 10 certified against a target of 10. Four of the cohort opted out of the exam in June, so the ceiling is now 6. The target was held rather than lowered, so the shortfall stays visible.
  • KR 3.1 stopped at 88%, not 100% — and 88% was the ceiling. Three weeks lost in June (3–23 Jun) could never be recovered; every week after 1 July was covered.

Five things the data showed

  • 1. The work moved outward, and the OKRs stayed pointed inward. Objective 1 measures Officience and closed at 58%; Objective 2 measures everything beyond it and closed at 77%. In August specifically, four sessions were delivered — three to PNV, one to the Impact Hub community — and none to Officience. Correcting KR 1.1's scope at close actually lowered its score, which is the clearest evidence of the shift: teaching capacity did not shrink, it relocated.
  • 2. Delegation restored output. It did not restore rhythm. Content collapsed for five weeks in June while Tam was building, and resumed only when authorship passed to Vũ Thị Thanh Thảo, who wrote 23 of the last 25 posts. That worked — weeks 18 to 24 were all covered. But the cycle still ended with a twelve-day silence after 20 August, the third pause in six months. The throughput problem was solved; the stop-start pattern was not.
  • 3. Learning converted into product, not into certificates — and the KR measuring it could not see that. Five CCAF courses became the Academy, the Brain, and the Unis bot. KR 1.2's written target is "automate 50% of social marketing workflow", so it scored 70% for a period of unusually productive learning. The KR measured the wrong axis all cycle.
  • 4. Reciprocity became the partnership model. VND 2m/month contributed back to Impact Hub, Olivier Lefèvre reframed from training buyer to ecosystem participant, Flow Game co-branded rather than sold. None of these score a point under a KR set built around "training programs delivered" — and they produced the strongest relationship UnIqueS has.
  • 5. KR 2.2 kept scoring while ceasing to inform. It reached 100% and, even after two items were excluded at close, still holds eight relationships against a target of five — at which point it stopped distinguishing a good month from a bad one.
  • 6. Activity sharing held its purpose while missing its unit. Post coverage widened from 1 week to 6 weeks per post, yet no period in the whole cycle went unreported. The weekly count was not met; the alignment the KR existed for was. That gap between unit and purpose is why the KR was rescored on coverage at close and its cadence formally moved to monthly.
