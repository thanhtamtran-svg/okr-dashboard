One month of research into "second brain" applied to organisational knowledge management. Presented in a sharing session with a bilingual reference document and a recorded demo.

🎓 UnIqueS Academy — academy.uniques.vn (shipped)

A learning platform built to support the CCAF journey, grounded in learning science and positioning the chatbot as a tutor rather than an answer machine. Nine principles including Productive Struggle First, Retrieval Practice, and Socratic mode. First module: Building with the Claude API. Prompt Evaluation was applied while designing the workflow, and a Definition of Done was created to hold the quality bar.

Next: modules beyond Module 1, after the Prompting session.
🛠 New SDLC — how AI changes each phase

Tam's way of instructing Claude Code shifted toward Scrum and software-development frameworks — more control over what AI suggests and over the consequences of its output. Research complete and documented in July. Reinforced by earning the PSM certificate: Tam now works as Product Owner to an AI "developer", with a Product Goal, a Product Backlog, a Definition of Done, and weekly retrospectives whose improvements feed the next iteration. Related public thinking: the "vibe coding vs agentic engineering" piece, arguing that without tests and evals it is vibe coding no matter how good the prompt.

Next: apply each phase to development with Claude · share the knowledge in August.
🌐 RO LLM Wiki — knowledge graph complete

Built to preserve and reorganise the knowledge of the global Reinventing Organizations (Teal) community. The knowledge graph was completed in July.

Next: share with the RO community in August.

Surfaced a new marketplace project idea, possibly built with Diane or others practising what they learn on the CCAF journey. Parking lot: a 20th-anniversary unconference, and a monthly podcast with French-based coaches and facilitators.

What's Working — Amplify

  • Delegation restored consistency, effort alone did not. Content stopped for five weeks while Tam was building. It resumed — and immediately ran for six straight weeks — once authorship passed to Thanh Thảo, who wrote 18 of the last 19 posts. This is the "build a system rather than do activities" move from the May retrospective, demonstrably working. It is the most transferable lesson of the period.
  • Series format compounds. The eight-part "Học trong thời AI / Learning in the AI Era" run, published first in Vietnamese and then in English, produced the most sustained output of the year and fed directly off one website essay. One piece of thinking, sixteen posts, two languages.
  • Learning is converting into product, not just certificates. The CCAF material did not stay as coursework — it became the Academy, the Brain, and the Unis bot. Prompt Evaluation was applied in a real design decision. This is a better return on the programme than the exam count suggests.
  • Content still generates inbound. Impact Hub found UnIqueS through LinkedIn in April and has since escalated to an APAC demo request. The flywheel from the May review is still turning.
  • Reciprocity is becoming the partnership model. Contributing VND 2m/month back to Impact Hub, and reframing Olivier from training partner to ecosystem participant, both point the same way: UnIqueS gets stronger by being a contributing member of communities rather than a vendor selling into them. The weekly co-branded Flow Game is the same instinct made concrete.

What Needs Attention

  • The content system rests on one person. Output collapsed when Tam was busy and recovered when Thanh Thảo took over. That is progress, but the dependency simply moved — it did not disappear. If Thanh Thảo is unavailable, the June pattern repeats.
  • The Diaries cadence stretched before it stopped. One week, one week, one week, two weeks, three weeks, then silence. Gradual widening is the failure mode that is hardest to notice from inside. Worth a trigger — e.g. "if a post covers more than 7 days, that is the warning" — rather than relying on intent.
  • CCAF lost 40% of its cohort. Four of ten opted out of the exam at the June check-in. The reason is not recorded anywhere. Before the next cycle, it is worth asking those four directly — the answer determines whether the programme design or the exam itself is the obstacle.
  • KR 2.1's pipeline thinned even as its score rose. Joyful Care, AI Resilience and CCIFV all closed. The number doubled while the underlying position narrowed. Two of the three remaining routes are undelivered.
  • The Diaries cadence widened again, to six weeks. The July post closed the calendar gap and is substantial — but it covers 26 Jun to 7 Aug in one piece. The sequence is now 1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 6 weeks. The score rose to 58%; whether the KR still does its job (weekly alignment) is a separate question from whether the box is ticked.

Reality Check — 24 days to 31 Aug

  • KR 1.1 cannot be met. 12 of 24 sessions. Reaching the target needs 12 more in 24 days, against an actual pace of 2.4/month. Not "behind" — arithmetically closed.
  • KR 2.3 cannot be fully met. With 4 confirmed opt-outs, at most 6 of 10 can certify — and the remaining learners said in June they need 1–2 more months. Nobody is likely to certify before 31 Aug.
  • KR 3.3 is already past its deadline (30 Jun, now 58%). The gap is closed but the ceiling is around 19 of 24.
  • KR 3.1 has one week left. The 24-week run that began 1 Mar ends 15 Aug, not 31 Aug. Maximum reachable is 21 of 24 (88%).
  • Genuinely still winnable in 24 days — KR 3.4. The Flow Game × Impact Hub weekly Saturday series is launched, public and co-branded. Running the first session takes this KR from 25% to 50%, and it is scheduled to recur. EUH is a second route. Of everything still short of target, this is the only point that is days away rather than months.
Three of nine KRs are now mathematically unreachable. That is worth stating plainly rather than managing around — but it is not the same as underperformance. Two KRs were fully met — KR 2.2 and KR 3.2 at 100%. The 24-week targets were designed in March for an organisation running activities. From 8 May onward the work shifted to building systems: the Brain, the Academy, the bot, delegated content, a signed partnership. That work is real, and it is largely invisible to the nine KRs measuring it.

Questions for the next cycle (Sep 2026 – Feb 2027)

  • Should KR 1.2 be rewritten rather than re-scored? It measures "automate 50% of social marketing workflow" — and the last three months delivered almost no automation but a great deal of learning-to-product conversion. The KR measured the wrong axis, so its 70% tells you nothing useful either way.
  • What does "engaged" mean for KR 2.2 next time? Under the current definition — a serious exchange — the target is met at 100% and the KR stops being informative. Under a stricter bar (co-hosted, or paid), the count is three. Which number would actually change a decision?
  • Should session count be a KR at all? KR 1.1 rewards volume. The highest-value work of this period — one 396-impression post, one signed partnership, one shipped Academy — would score zero under it.
  • How does AI-native get measured without becoming another unreachable count? The fourth objective has no targets by design. Before setting any, it is worth deciding what "AI-native" would look like in evidence: fewer manual hours? more team members using the Brain weekly? the bot answering questions a person used to answer?
  • Who else can publish? The single-person dependency has moved once already. A third author, or a repeatable format someone else can run, is the difference between a system and a substitution.
  • Is KR 2.1 measuring the strategy UnIqueS is leaving behind? "4 external training programs delivered" is a vendor's metric. July's two clearest decisions — Olivier into the ecosystem, VND 2m/month into Impact Hub — are contributor moves, and neither scores a single point under the current KR set. If the positioning is now ecosystem contributor, the next cycle needs a KR that can see it.
  • Should KR 3.3 measure posts, or measure alignment? A six-week summary ticks the box a weekly sync was meant to serve. If the real goal is that the team stays in sync without spending two hours a week in meetings — which is what Duc praised these posts for — then post count may be the wrong proxy.
